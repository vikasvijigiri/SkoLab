# Curriculum Vitae Template (Tenure-Track Academic)

An academic CV is a complete record, ordered so the first page carries the
information a search committee weighs most. Unlike a resume, length is not
a concern — completeness and consistent formatting are. Keep entries
reverse-chronological within each section.

## 1. Contact and identifiers
Name, institutional affiliation, email, and your ORCID / Google Scholar /
lab website. Include a personal website only if it's substantive.

## 2. Education
Degree, field, institution, location, year conferred. Include dissertation
title and advisor for your doctorate; list qualifying-exam or candidacy
dates only if your field's convention expects it.

## 3. Academic appointments
Title, department, institution, and date range for every position —
postdoc, visiting appointments, current role. Distinguish research-only
from teaching-and-research appointments if that distinction exists in your
field.

## 4. Publications
Split into clearly labeled subsections matching your field's norms, e.g.:
- Peer-reviewed journal articles
- Peer-reviewed conference papers
- Book chapters / edited volumes
- Preprints / working papers (clearly marked as not yet peer-reviewed)
Full citation for each (all authors, in author order, venue, year, and DOI
or link). Bold or underline your own name for multi-author work. Note
equal-contribution co-authorship explicitly where it applies — don't bury
it in a footnote a skimming reviewer will miss.

## 5. Grants and fellowships
Funding source, program name, amount, dates, and your role (PI / Co-PI /
Senior Personnel). Include both awarded and, in a clearly separate
subsection, pending/submitted proposals — never blend the two.

## 6. Teaching experience
Course title, level (undergrad/grad), institution, and term. Note
instructor-of-record versus TA roles distinctly.

## 7. Mentorship
Students supervised (name optional per your field's convention), degree
level, and outcome (thesis title, current position if known).

## 8. Invited talks and presentations
Venue, title, and date. Separate invited talks from conference
presentations of your own submitted work — they signal different things to
a committee.

## 9. Service
Journal/conference reviewing, program committees, departmental or
university committees, editorial roles. Group by type.

## 10. Honors and awards
Reverse-chronological, with the awarding body named.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- Inconsistent citation formatting between sections.
- Mixing pending and awarded grants in one list.
- Omitting DOIs/links, forcing a reader to search for your own work.
- A CV that hasn't been updated to reflect your most recent 6-12 months.
